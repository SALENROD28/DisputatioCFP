What CCCC Provided

An Incomplete History

CCCC was founded in 1949 within the National Council of Teachers of English (NCTE) by Dr. John C. Gerber. Since its incipient, the conference has met annually through 2026, when it was de-charted. CCCC published the first issue of College Composition and Communication (CCC) in 1950 which kick started a long history of research and scholarship. The journal publishes research and scholarship in rhetoric and composition studies that supports college teachers in reflecting on and improving their writing instruction. This work reflects current scholarship and theory in the field. Areas of scholarship include but not limited to, English studies, rhetoric, cultural studies, communication sociology and more. CCCC has three different publication outlets: College Composition and Communication, Studies in Writing and Rhetoric (SWR), and FORUM.

On top of publishing, CCCC has held their annual conference every year since 1950 when the first conference appeared in Chicago, Illinois. With each conference, the host city also offered options for housing near the conference so presenters can seemingly make their way to the conference without any issues. The conference is held in person with the options such as Ignite Sessions, Engaged Learning Experience Sessions, Poster Sessions, Workshop Sessions, Member Groups: SIGs and Standing Groups, and CCCC Documentaries. These wide range of sessions allows for different modalities that a presenter can choose how they’d like their research to be presented. Additionally, CCCC has “Area Clusters” which a presenter can choose from when its comes to the focus of their research. When the COVID-19 Pandemic hit the world, CCCC had to cancel one conference but was able to find a solution on how presenters can still present just not in person. This is where CCCC started to allow virtual presentations which helped out not only because the previous conference was canceled but others who can’t afford to travel but could still present virtually.

Forming the Center

In his review of Views from the Center: The CCCC Chairs' Addresses, 1977–2005 Dr. David A. Jolliffe (2007) recounts a Duan Roen edited collection which was published by Bedford/St. Martin’s. Jolliffe explains that Roen “has done exactly what a good editor is supposed to do with a collection” (449). Jolliffe highlights Roen’s approach to introducing the volume succinctly, “and clearly and then let the original texts, which are reprinted in their revised versions that appeared in College Composition and Communication, speak for themselves” (449). The review discusses the importance of marginalized communities, a concern that connects to CCCC’s goals and mission through 2026. The Conference on College Composition & Communication mission statement has supported commits to agency, power, and potential of diverse communicators inside and outside classrooms.

Teaching

CCCC has offered funding for scholars and students through opportunities such as travel grants. Additionally, its awards recognize exemplary work in the field. Grants and Awards offered through CCCC include Leadership and Program Awards, Publication and Scholarship Awards, Research Grants, and Travel Awards. These opportunities serve different purposes, from assisting with conference fees and travel to support research and contributions to the field. For example, the Exemplar Award “recognizes an individual who has served as an exemplar for our organization.” CCCC also supports teaching and writing programs that include, Outstanding Teacher Award, Writing Program of Excellence Award, and Tribal College Faculty Fellowship. These opportunities support graduate students and teachers while recognizing their scholarship in the field.

Disciplinary Identity

Through CCCC, they offer Position Statements, Guidelines, and Resources covering a variety of education issues “vital to the teaching and learning of writing” They also note that these statements can include implied suggestions for putting recommendations to practice. These are then passed down to Leadership that consists of the CCCC Executive Committee. Additionally, CCCC has a rotating chair in which Assistant Chair, which is a 4-year term to serve through the CCCC Chairs’, consists of commitments for Travel and Time, and Institutional Support.

Our Purpose and Goals

Our webtext responds to the recent de-chartering of CCCC and asks contributors to consider how scholars in writing studies are making sense of this moment, particularly in relation to disciplinary identity, disagreement, coalition building, and the future of the field. Rather than debating whether de-charting was right or wrong, we argue that the field must reconsider the kinds of disciplinary infrastructure we should provide moving forward. Dr. Johnson’s involvement with CCCC provides an important perspective on the organization's evolution and the subsequent events leading to its de-charting. While I didn’t have extensive involvement with CCCC like Dr. Johnson, my perspective touches on the many people involved with the building of the conference and my experience as an attendee. Together, our different positions allow us to consider not just the de-charting of CCCC but also what we as a field can create, support and what we can build for the future.
